The record

Three decades,
five bands

Paul Wisner has played guitar in Boston-area original-music rock bands for over three decades, and synthesizer in two of them. He has played on published albums in every era. Songwriting came late: he did almost no writing until Miskatonic and Ghosts and Shadows—before that, the only song he wrote was “Mary” in Ultrabreakfast.

This history gathers every gig date, venue, recording project and album publication that could be verified from his email, Facebook posts, press, label and streaming listings, and band pages.

How to read the gig lists. Each band has one gig list in date order, merging confirmed dates with announced listings from every source. For the 1990s bands, club calendars were rarely archived online. Announced listings are marked as announced, not confirmed played. Where only a venue run is documented, venues are listed without inventing dates. Later Ghosts and Shadows dates, and Miskatonic’s 2010–2011 calendar, come from Paul’s own booking mail and announcements.

The through-line

The same Boston club circuit recurs for thirty years: Green Street Grill, T.T. the Bear’s Place, the Middle East, the Paradise, Avalon, Harper’s Ferry, Lizard Lounge, Midway Cafe, O’Brien’s Pub and, later, Deep Cuts, Square Root and the Jungle.

Bandmates recur too: Johnny and Nicole Anguish link Calendar Girl to Stever; Liz Firger, Steve Kelly and Tim Farrell link Miskatonic back to Calendar Girl and Ultrabreakfast; Gabriella Lawrence, Steve Reynolds and Dean Fisher carry the story into Ghosts and Shadows.

Calendar Girl

1998–2002 · archival release 2025

Formed in late 1997 when Wisner and Anguish—co-workers with the same CDs on their desks—decided to play together. The band began playing live in January 1998.

Lineup Johnny Anguish, guitar/vocals · Paul Wisner, guitar/vocals · Eric Krauter, bass/vocals · Dave Foy, drums

By their third show, “King of Bad Luck” was getting college and local-music-show airplay; at the end of 1998 it was in WZBC’s Top 90.3 of 1998.

Miskatonic

2007–2011 · reunion 2026

A potent mixture of new wave and rock and roll: ’80s synth and bass, punky guitar textures and Liz Firger’s melodic vocals.

Lineup Liz Firger, lead vocals · Tim Farrell, guitar/vocals · Paul Wisner, guitar/synth/vocals · Steve Kelly, bass · Mark Patti, drumsName From H. P. Lovecraft’s Miskatonic University.

The band’s bio credits melodies to Tim Farrell and Paul Wisner, describing “two years of song writing” informed by the Cars and the Pixies. This is where Paul’s songwriting began in earnest.

What Daykamp Music said

“Toss The Cars and the B-52s in a blender. Add a dash of Fountains of Wayne and a smidge of The Rentals. Voila! Miskatonic.”

The July 2011 review of Let Us Entertain Us highlights Paul’s synth melodies on “Sno-Cone Girl” and “Extra Terrestrial Girl,” Elizabeth Firger’s vocal on “The Fall,” Steve Kelly’s melodic bass on “The Extra Mile,” and Tim Farrell’s delivery on “Dregs Of The World.” The October review calls “Favorite Records” infectious, “Sudden Silence” a song with “a cool ending,” and notes Firger’s increased confidence across the EP.

History

The band was founded by Gabriella Lawrence, Paul Wisner, KSushi and Steve Reynolds at a Halloween party. Paul had been in Miskatonic; Gabriella had briefly been a member too. Associated projects include Juliana Hatfield Three, Dylan in the Movies, Cheater Pint and Tinkertown, plus links to Miskatonic, Calendar Girl, Ultrabreakfast and Stever Must Lead the Rebellion.

Bibliography &
research guide

A working map of where Boston club-era evidence survives—and what each source can and cannot prove.

The core rule: a listing is not a gig, airplay is not a gig, and a database date is not automatically the band’s date. Keep announced, played and released separate until independent sources agree.
Five-step method
StepDo thisWhy
1List every spelling, former name and side project.Databases index one spelling; names can collide.
2List every member and their other bands.Bandmates are the bridges between archives.
3Build the discography first.Release dates anchor the timeline and cluster gigs.
4Hunt gigs by venue and year.Club calendars rarely survive indexed by band.
5Save each URL, capture date and image/PDF.Sites vanish and domains get hijacked.

The Boston Phoenix, 1973–2013

The single richest Boston source: club ads, weekly Cellars columns, reviews and scene reporting. Search the Internet Archive collection by band, member and regular venue. Display ads may need visual reading because OCR handles small listings better than artwork.

Music Museum of New England overview

The Wayback Machine

Start here for dead band sites, MySpace pages, label pages and venue calendars. The Calendar Girl gig log survives only in archived calendargirlrock.com captures; Miskatonic’s early gigs survive on archived MySpace. Read captures across several years and save timestamps.

Internet Archive’s Boston collection notes

Northeastern University archives

Snell Library holds the physical Phoenix archives, sister papers, WFNX records and the Boston Globe Library Collection. It is the place to look for unpublished photos and print reviews that were never digitized.

Archives and Special Collections contact

College and community radio

WMBR’s playlist database logs individual plays across decades; its weekly concert report and Wayback captures can recover listings. A play proves a recording existed and reached a DJ by that date—not that a gig happened. Also search WZBC, WMFO, WERS and WHRB.

Gig and setlist databases

setlist.fm and Concert Archives are useful cross-checks, but an absence proves nothing. Also search archived venue calendars, promoter mail and festival archives such as the WBCN Rumble, CMJ and Emergenza.

Concert Archives

Live recordings

The Live Music Archive preserves audience tapes that prove a show happened and can yield a setlist. YouTube is the video equivalent; the earliest moving image in this history survives through the Billy Ruane archive.

Live Music Archive background · Internet Archive blog

Release and credit databases

Discogs for pressings, labels and catalogue numbers; verify against scans. MusicBrainz for people, aliases and identifiers. AllMusic for biographies and credits, with caution on dates. Bandcamp for release timestamps and liner notes; capture what you see.

MusicBrainz background

Local press and scene memory

Beyond the Phoenix: The Noise, Boston Groupie News, the Boston Globe, college newspapers such as the Tufts Daily, and scene blogs such as Daykamp Music. Search every member name, not only the band.

Your own and your bandmates’ archives

Export and search email and social archives. Ask bandmates. Scan flyers, posters and ticket stubs, then use text recognition. Private booking mail resolved several venue errors in this history. If no evidence survives, say so.

Rules:
- Only report gigs, releases, and facts you can source. Cite the source for each.
- When sources conflict (e.g. two different release dates), report all versions and flag the conflict instead of picking one.
- Never invent a gig, release, or review. If you can’t verify it, say so.
- Ask me to confirm anything only I would know (lineup changes, unreleased material) rather than guessing.
